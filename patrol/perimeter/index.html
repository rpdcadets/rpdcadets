    How the picture is drawn

    Containment ring: K9 containment doctrine, 2 to 3 blocks for every minute since the suspect was last seen (2.5 by default), using the block size measured from the streets around the pin. Likely hiding area: most urban foot suspects go to ground within about 4 blocks of where they were last seen (Calgary Police dog-section study). Direction wedge: about half of fleeing or lost subjects are found within 30 degrees of their last direction of travel and three quarters within 66 (search-and-rescue dispersion data). Unaware suspects (a caller saw them, no police contact) are drawn at walking pace, 4 mph by default, with no hiding area, because they keep moving at a normal pace. Vehicle reach: travel time over the road network at posted speeds from OpenRouteService; a fleeing driver can beat it. Addresses come first from the City of Richardson GIS address points. Spots: the loop of streets that encloses the reach plus a block (LAPD Air Support containment course: set the four corners first, then mid-block; never a half-block perimeter; it is easier to tighten than to expand), up to six: the corners, then the mid-block posts that cover the most line.
